# Portfolio Master Brief

## Objective

Build a distinctive creative-engineering portfolio that feels authored rather than templated. It should communicate strong product/frontend engineering, visual judgment, broad systems awareness, and an AI-native direction without presenting the owner as an ML researcher.

The site should feel more like a designed digital object than a conventional developer portfolio.

## Homepage structure

1. Hero
2. Selected Work
3. What I Build
4. Lab
5. Contact / Footer

Do not re-add conventional About, Process, Timeline, Testimonial, Resume, Tech Stack, or Experience sections to the homepage unless there is a very strong reason. Those were intentionally removed because they weakened the page and made it feel more like a portfolio template.

## Hero

The hero is already a signature area and should be protected.

- Large condensed `CREATIVE ENGINEER` headline.
- Soft blue/pastel environment.
- Sculptural 3D geometric/mechanical hero object.
- Small technical descriptor copy.
- Clear CTA into the work.
- Optional grounding descriptor such as `DESIGN / ENGINEERING / SYSTEMS / AI`.
- Minimal navigation and availability status.

Do not overload the hero with dashboards, terminals, AI particles, glass UI, or unnecessary interaction.

## Selected Work

Treat projects as individually art-directed pieces, not repeated cards populated from one template.

- Maintain the horizontally expansive/gallery feeling.
- Allow a partially visible next project when appropriate.
- Different projects may use different internal composition while sharing the same outer design system.
- Real project/case-study content should ultimately replace placeholder projects.
- Clicking a project should reveal technical proof: problem, role, constraints, system, decisions, implementation, and result.

## What I Build

This is the main interactive chapter of the homepage.

The title should not be a full-height card and must not mechanically match the height of the scrolling content. The title is sticky, compact, intentionally asymmetric, and at most two lines.

Current capability set:

- Creative Development
- Full-Stack Websites
- Digital Experiences
- AI-Driven Development
- Technical Direction

Each capability appears as a substantial/thick pill when inactive. As it reaches the active scroll position, the pill expands into a larger content canvas that reveals a concise explanation and the tools/technologies that support that capability.

Technology is therefore contextual rather than presented as a separate badge/logo section.

## Lab

Lab is not another portfolio section full of polished case studies. It is a doorway into experiments, unfinished ideas, technical explorations, smaller builds, AI/agent work, Go/backend experiments, prototypes, writing, and things being tested.

The core distinction:

- Selected Work = what has been proven/shipped.
- What I Build = areas of capability/ownership.
- Lab = curiosity, experimentation, and where the work is heading.

Lab should live inside a pastel container or visual field so it belongs to the homepage system. Use `LAB`, not `LAB.` — the period was intentionally rejected.

## Footer

Keep the coral contact/footer section as the strong visual ending.

- Large contact CTA.
- Dark high-contrast button.
- Contact details and social links.
- Availability status where relevant.
- 3D/sculptural visual may be used if it does not make the page overly dependent on 3D objects.

## Page rhythm

The homepage needs visible vertical separation between major sections. Do not stack Hero, Projects, What I Build, Lab, and Footer tightly against each other.

Use generous negative space so each chapter reads as intentional and independent. Desktop section gaps should feel noticeably larger than ordinary component spacing; tune responsively rather than relying on a single fixed pixel value.
